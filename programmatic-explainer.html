Foundation

What is Programmatic?

Programmatic advertising is the automated buying and selling of digital ad inventory using software, data, and algorithms — replacing the traditional manual process of negotiations, insertion orders, and phone calls between buyers and sellers.

Think of it as a stock exchange for ad impressions. Every time a webpage loads, an auction fires. Buyers bid based on who the user is. The highest bidder wins. All of this happens before the page finishes loading.

🤖

AUTOMATED

No manual negotiations. Software executes thousands of buying decisions per second, 24/7, across millions of websites simultaneously.

MACHINE SPEED
📊

DATA-DRIVEN

Every bid is informed by audience data — who the user is, what they've browsed, their demographics, intent signals, and more.

AUDIENCE FIRST
⚡

REAL-TIME

Auctions happen per impression, per user, in real time. You pay for who sees your ad, not just where it appears.

RTB
🎯

OPTIMIZABLE

Algorithms continuously learn from performance signals — CTR, conversions, viewability — and shift spend toward what works.

ML POWERED

Key shift: Traditional media buying was site-centric — "I want to be on ESPN.com." Programmatic is audience-centric — "I want to reach in-market SUV buyers, wherever they are across the internet."

The Programmatic Ecosystem

Programmatic runs on a layered tech stack. Each layer has a specific role, and data flows between all of them in milliseconds.

🏢

ADVERTISER / BRAND

The buyer. Wants to reach specific audiences and drive measurable outcomes (awareness, leads, purchases).

DEMAND SIDE
🖥️

DSP — Demand Side Platform

The advertiser's buying interface. Sets targeting, bids, budgets, creatives. Examples: DV360, The Trade Desk, Amazon DSP, Meta Advantage+.

BUY SIDE TECH
🔄

AD EXCHANGE

The marketplace where buyers and sellers meet. Runs the auction. Examples: Google Ad Exchange (AdX), OpenX, Magnite.

MARKETPLACE
📰

The publisher's monetization tool. Packages and sells their inventory to exchanges. Examples: GAM, Pubmatic, Index Exchange.

SELL SIDE TECH
🗂️

DMP / CDP

Data Management / Customer Data Platform. Aggregates and segments audience data (1st, 2nd, 3rd party) to inform bidding decisions.

DATA LAYER
🌐

PUBLISHER / WEBSITE

The inventory owner. Has ad slots to fill. Connects to SSP to maximize revenue from their audience and content.

The Auction

Real-Time Bidding — What Actually Happens

A user lands on a page. In the time it takes to blink, here's what fires:

Step 01

Page Load

User visits a publisher's site. The page begins to load.

t = 0ms
Step 02

Bid Request

SSP fires a bid request to ad exchanges with user data, ad slot specs, floor price.

t = 5ms
Step 03

DSP Evaluation

DSP receives the request, checks targeting rules, and calculates a bid using algorithms + data.

t = 20ms
Step 04

Auction

Exchange receives all bids. 2nd-price auction: winner pays $0.01 above 2nd-highest bid.

t = 80ms
Step 05

Ad Served

Winning ad creative is fetched and rendered in the user's browser.

t = 100ms

Header Bidding: A newer evolution where the publisher sends bid requests to multiple exchanges simultaneously (not sequentially), maximizing competition and yield. Most major publishers use header bidding today via Prebid.js.

2ND PRICE AUCTION (Classic)

Winner pays $0.01 above the second-highest bid. Incentivizes true-value bidding. e.g. A bids $5, B bids $3 → A wins, pays $3.01.

1ST PRICE AUCTION (Modern)

Winner pays their exact bid. Now common since 2019 industry shift. Requires smarter bid shading — DSPs like DV360 do this automatically.

Deal Structures

Programmatic Buying Types

Not all programmatic is open-market bidding. Here's the full spectrum of how inventory is transacted:

🔓 Open Auction (Open RTB)

+

The default programmatic marketplace. Any eligible buyer can participate. Highest bidder wins.

  • Pro: Massive scale. Access to billions of impressions across any publisher.
  • Pro: Lowest CPMs typically.
  • Con: Less control over premium placements. Brand safety risks.
  • Con: Higher fraud risk vs. private deals.

Best for: broad awareness, prospecting, performance campaigns needing scale.

🔒 Private Marketplace (PMP)

+

An invitation-only auction. Publisher creates a curated deal with a select group of buyers. Buyers bid with a Deal ID within the PMP.

  • Pro: Access to premium, brand-safe publisher inventory.
  • Pro: More transparency — you know the exact sites/apps.
  • Con: Higher floor prices. Less scale than open auction.

Best for: brand campaigns, premium publishers (NYT, Forbes), vertical-specific placements.

🤝 Preferred Deal

+

A 1:1 negotiated deal between advertiser and publisher at a fixed CPM. Buyer gets first look at the inventory before it goes to open auction, but isn't obligated to buy.

  • Pro: Fixed pricing. First-look advantage.
  • Pro: Relationship-driven — you know exactly who you're buying from.
  • Con: No guaranteed volume. Publisher may not always have matching inventory.

📋 Programmatic Guaranteed (PG)

+

Automated direct buying with reserved inventory. Combines the guarantees of traditional direct deals with programmatic automation. No auction — it's a reservation.

  • Pro: Guaranteed impressions and placement. Great for launches, sponsorships.
  • Pro: Programmatic workflow — no IO paperwork, automated trafficking.
  • Con: Premium CPMs. Must negotiate volume upfront.
  • In DV360: set up under Inventory → Deals → Programmatic Guaranteed.

Case Study — DSP

DV360 — Google's DSP Explained

DISPLAY & VIDEO 360

DV360 (formerly DoubleClick Bid Manager) is Google's enterprise-grade Demand Side Platform. It's the buy-side tool in Google's marketing stack, connected to Google Ad Exchange, YouTube, and hundreds of third-party exchanges.

DV360 is where programmatic strategy meets execution for large advertisers and agencies. Here's how its architecture maps to programmatic theory:

🏛️

PARTNER / ADVERTISER

Top-level account structure. Partner = agency (e.g. Publicis). Advertiser = individual brand client. All campaigns sit under an advertiser.

ACCOUNT LEVEL
📁

CAMPAIGN

Budget, flight dates, frequency caps, KPI goals (e.g. target CPA, target ROAS, maximize impressions). Sets the overarching objective.

STRATEGY LEVEL
🎯

INSERTION ORDER (IO)

Represents a budget allocation within a campaign. Has its own pacing, spend targets, and can hold multiple line items.

PLANNING LEVEL
📋

LINE ITEM (LI)

The execution layer. This is where targeting, bidding strategy, frequency, creatives, and inventory sources are set. The most granular lever.

EXECUTION LEVEL

Bidding Strategies in DV360

FIXED BID

You manually set a CPM/CPC. No automation. You control exactly what you pay per impression. Good for PMPs, testing, or guaranteed deals.

TARGET CPA

Algo targets a specific cost per conversion. Requires conversion data. DV360 auto-adjusts bids based on predicted conversion probability.

MAXIMIZE CLICKS

Spend full budget while maximizing click volume. Useful for traffic campaigns without hard conversion goals.

VIEWABLE IMPRESSION

Bids to maximize Active View impressions (MRC standard: 50% in view for 1 second). Great for brand awareness KPIs.

DV360 vs Google Ads: Google Ads is a self-serve platform for search + display with limited targeting depth. DV360 is an enterprise DSP with access to Google AdX, YouTube, third-party exchanges, first-party data integration (via PAIR), cross-channel frequency management, and programmatic guaranteed deals. DV360 also has deeper DMP/CDP integration and advanced measurement via Floodlight tags.

How We Measure Programmatic

Programmatic's true advantage is measurability. Every impression, click, and conversion is trackable and attributable.

VTRView-Through Rate

📐 Viewability (Active View)

+

MRC standard: an ad is "viewable" if 50% of its pixels are in-view for at least 1 continuous second (2 seconds for video).

  • DV360 reports Active View metrics natively.
  • Industry benchmark: 70%+ viewability is considered good for display.
  • Viewable CPM (vCPM) pricing is increasingly common for brand campaigns.

🔄 Attribution Models

+
  • Last Click: 100% credit to final click. Undervalues upper-funnel programmatic.
  • View-Through Attribution: Credits impressions that weren't clicked but preceded a conversion. Standard in DV360 (30-day window by default).
  • Data-Driven Attribution (DDA): ML model distributes credit across touchpoints based on their actual contribution. Available in DV360 via CM360 integration.
  • Floodlight Tags: Google's cross-channel tracking pixel used across DV360, CM360, and SA360. The source of truth for conversions in the Google stack.

🧪 Incrementality & Brand Lift

+

Beyond last-click: did the campaign actually drive incremental results?

  • Brand Lift Study (DV360/YouTube): Measures ad recall, awareness, consideration via survey-based holdout testing.
  • Conversion Lift: A/B holdout experiment measuring actual conversion uplift from exposed vs. unexposed users.
  • Media Mix Modeling (MMM): Statistical model attributing revenue contribution to each channel, accounting for saturation and lag effects.
The Dark Side

Programmatic Challenges

Programmatic is powerful but comes with a set of persistent challenges every practitioner must navigate:

🤖

AD FRAUD & IVT

Invalid traffic (bots, click farms, domain spoofing) inflates impression counts without real humans. Industry loses ~$100B/year. Mitigation: IAS, DoubleVerify, ads.txt, SIVT filtering.

BRAND RISK
🎭

BRAND SAFETY

Ads appearing next to harmful, controversial, or inappropriate content. Open auction = less control. Solution: blocklists, inclusion lists, brand safety vendors (IAS, DV).

REPUTATION
🍪

COOKIE DEPRECATION

3rd-party cookies are fading. Chrome's Privacy Sandbox, iOS tracking restrictions (ATT) are reshaping audience targeting. Shift to 1P data and contextual is critical.

INDUSTRY SHIFT
📦

AD STACKING / STUFFING

Multiple ads stacked invisibly on top of each other — publisher charges for all, only top one is seen. Viewability measurement helps expose this.

FRAUD
💰

SUPPLY CHAIN OPACITY

The "ad tech tax" — only ~50 cents of every dollar reaches the publisher. Multiple intermediaries each take a cut. Advertisers push for transparency via ads.txt and supply path optimization (SPO).

COST EFFICIENCY
😤

AD FATIGUE

Poor frequency management leads to overexposure — burning brand equity and wasting budget. DV360's cross-channel frequency management helps enforce caps.
